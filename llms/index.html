LLMs

It is 2025 and I guess I have to write my thoughts on LLMs.

This post isn’t here to start a discussion, or an invitation to get feedback or hear opinions. It is here for me to have a quick link when I inevitably have to “explain” my views, and maybe in case someone needs to read this.

The Environmental Destruction

I start here because really this is enough of a problem on its own to close the chapter on LLMs.

The environmental cost of LLMs is extreme.
And in a world heading ever faster towards an ecosystem unsuitable for human life LLMs are wasting the precious years we have left to undo the damage of the past while doubling down on the mistakes.

The environmental impact of data centers is not new, and far too little has been done about this in the past decades, mainly expecting “the hardware people” to fix it all with ever more efficient buildings and servers while software becomes ever more bloated and inefficient.

But the consumption of LLMs took things to a new level.
And this is active harm we do to people’s lives, often our owns, that can’t be justified away with hand-wavy “it will be fixed eventually” because the eventually is scheduled some decades after most of the planet has become uninhabitable for most of us.

The Creative Value Gaslight

Just because the environmental problems are enough to dismiss LLMs it does not mean they are the only reason to do so.

For me, the next big thing is the devastation of the creative world.
By creative world here I refer to everything that requires imagination, prospective or insight to make: from book authors to artisans crafting unique and imaginative works.

This process of creation has been stolen by LLMs makers and used with complete disregard with content licensing and Copyright laws.
You won’t often find me defending Copyright laws: the idea of protecting author rights is good and fair but copyright laws don’t actually do that. They are a tool most used by distributors, often large and wealthy, to control content regardless of what authors or buyers want.

To add insult to injury, creative work has been greatly devalued in society. In many education systems, humanities are seen as inferior topics to sciences. And at least in my case the value of learning these lessons wasn’t even really explained: you just memorize things because you are told to do so.

Yet now (self proclaimed) multi billion USD companies are claiming creative content is so essential they can’t be stopped from using them or LLMs could never exist or improve … You have to chose: either these works are not valuable, so loosing them should not matter, or the are valuable, so you should properly compensate those who created them.

The Devaluing of knowledge

I work as a Software Engineer and I’ve evolved to believe that the core of my job is not the code I write but the knowledge I use to write it and the knowledge I use to not write code.

Knowledge is much more then the collection of facts in our individual and collective minds. And it is more then the connections between those facts. I believe knowledge is the force we use to internalise these facts and connections. To know how and why pulling at one thread has ripple effects on everything connected to it. It is the skill to build understanding and to use that understanding to build more knowledge in return.

LLMs are incapable of doing this, but appear to achieve the same results without building any of that understating. And without the ability to derive new understanding from existing knowledge. When used as advertised, LLMs have been shown to quickly erode the skills of their users without actually replacing these skills with anything more then a life-long subscription.

And many experts have written in detail about how the current LLMs cannot evolve into something that can handle real knowledge.

The BuiltIn Unreliability

LLMs are probabilistic tools that put words next to each other. They don’t understand or know, they only figure out how likely it is for any word to be next, and pick the highest probabilities. Modern LLMs can do this more then one word at a time, but it is still all they do.

This is where “hallucinations” come from: the big data scrape used as input made it look probabilistically likely even though they are nonsense. The reality though is that “hallucinations” aren’t exactly a bug: they are a direct results of the core function and so can’t be eliminated.

It Also Doesn’t Work (For Me)

I find it absurd when having conversations on the topics above that people respond with something along the lines of “it works” …

But even this isn’t true for me. I’ve tried LLMs when I was required to, and I see people use them and their results.

In Conclusion

I’ve spoken too much about this nonsense. LLMs are not for me and I can’t imagine using them given the active harm they cause.
